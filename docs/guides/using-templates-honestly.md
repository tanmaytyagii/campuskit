# Using templates honestly

CampusKit templates are complete, working projects that you can learn from and build on. Starting from a template is a legitimate way to learn, in the same way that starting from a textbook example is, as long as you are open about it and follow your institution's rules.

## What the licence allows, and what it doesn't

Every CampusKit template is released under the MIT License. It lets you use, copy, modify and share the code, including in projects you submit, as long as you keep the copyright and licence notice (the `LICENSE` file) with the code.

The licence is about copyright. It does not:

- make you the author of code you did not write;
- override your institution's academic integrity rules;
- tell you whether a template is allowed for a particular assignment.

If your course requires that you write the project from scratch, a template is not appropriate, whatever its licence says. If you are unsure, ask your guide or teacher **before** you start.

## Do

- **Say where you started.** In your report and your viva, name the template and its version. For example: “This project started from the CampusKit _Student Attendance System_ template (version 1.0.0, MIT License).” The generated `GETTING_STARTED.md` records the template and version for you.
- **Keep the `LICENSE` file** in the project, and keep the template's attribution in the README.
- **Make real changes,** and describe them precisely: features you added, screens you redesigned, tests you wrote, bugs you fixed.
- **Measure your own results.** Use screenshots you captured, test results you ran and numbers you measured on your own computer.
- **Understand every part** you present, including the parts you didn't change. The [viva guide](prepare-for-your-viva.md) shows how.

## Don't

- Don't remove the licence or the attribution, or present the template as entirely your own work.
- Don't copy the drafts from the report builder without rewriting them. They are prompts and facts from the project's documentation, not finished text, and they are not checked against your institution's rules.
- Don't put results, test outcomes, citations or screenshots in your report that you did not produce yourself.
- Don't submit the same template as a classmate without both of you making it your own.

## Changing the project's name and branding

The generator writes your project name, institution and other choices into the files it declares, such as the app's title and the README. This is a convenience. It does not change who wrote the code, and the template's attribution stays in the README and the `LICENSE` file.
